# Course 2025–2026

Since 2025 I have been working as a **Technical Instructor** at EMT Granollers, teaching subjects related to mechanical design, manufacturing processes, industrial systems and product development.
My role combines **technical expertise**, **project‑based learning**, and **hands‑on workshop guidance**, helping students understand how engineering concepts translate into real industrial applications.

This page presents a complete overview of the modules I teach, the methodologies I apply, and the technical competencies students develop throughout the academic year.

---

# 0) Workshop — Industrial Machines & Troubleshooting

The workshop is where I carry out the most hands‑on part of my work, dealing daily with **three‑phase industrial machines** and their power and control systems. My main responsibility is to ensure that all machines used by the students operate safely and reliably, solving real issues that appear due to their intensive use.

A large part of my work focuses on **three‑phase power supply**, verifying the correct connection of the three phases, checking protections, ensuring continuity, and confirming that power circuits behave as expected. I also work on the **control logic**, diagnosing and manipulating:

- Contactors and relays
- Push buttons and limit switches
- Thermal protections and fuses
- Manual and automatic start/stop systems

A significant portion of my role involves **fault detection and troubleshooting**. Many issues are caused by worn cables, loose terminals, damaged connectors, or incorrect manipulation by students. These situations have allowed me to develop strong skills in:

- Electrical and mechanical diagnostics
- Reading and interpreting industrial schematics
- Verifying power and control circuits
- Performing quick, safe and effective repairs

This experience has given me a very realistic understanding of industrial maintenance and machine behaviour in an educational environment, where student errors create a wide variety of real‑world problems.

# 1) Manufacturing Project — Mechanical Eccentricity Gauge

This module is one of the most complete and demanding of the academic year.
All students must **design and manufacture a mechanical eccentricity gauge** (“mediador d’excentricitats mecàniques”), a precision tool used to measure deviations in rotating components.

The objective is to develop the full set of competencies required to **design, plan, manage and evaluate a complete mechanical manufacturing project**, exactly as it would be done in an industrial environment.

## Project Deliverables

Each student must produce:

- **Technical report (Memòria)**
- **Complete technical drawings (Planos)**
- **Specifications document (Plec de condicions)**
- **Budget and cost analysis (Pressupost)**
- **Manufactured physical part**, produced in the workshop

## Skills Developed

### 1. Initial analysis and market study
- Benchmarking existing tools
- Understanding industrial requirements

### 2. Project definition and planning
- Pre‑project documentation
- Patent and prior‑art research
- Concept sketches and prototypes
- Gantt charts and planning tools

### 3. Conceptual and detailed design
- Design thinking
- CAD modelling
- Tolerances and fits
- Material selection
- Safety and ergonomics

### 4. Technical and economic management
- Cost estimation
- Quality control
- Manufacturing process selection

### 5. Safety, hygiene and quality
- Risk assessment
- Safe machining practices

### 6. Industrial project management methodologies
- Waterfall vs. Agile
- Lean principles
- Continuous improvement

## Outcome

By the end of the module, students have designed, documented and manufactured a precision tool, experiencing the full workflow from idea to final product.

# 2) Mechanical Product Design Project — Industry‑Based Projects

This module shares the same structure and rigor as the previous one, but with a key difference:
**each student develops a project based on their real work placement (FCT) environment.**

Instead of manufacturing the final product in the workshop, the focus is on **advanced engineering development**, applying theory to real industrial problems.

## Example Student Projects

- Electronic variable‑speed drive
- Automated smart warehouse
- Industrial concrete mixer
- Paintball pressure tank
- Plastic shredder and extruder
- Coffee vending machine
- Two‑stroke engine redesign
- Stamping die (matriu)
- SCARA robot

## Project Development Structure

### 1. Problem definition and industrial context
- Understanding company needs
- Identifying constraints
- Analysing existing systems

### 2. Functional analysis
- Requirements
- Performance criteria
- Functional diagrams

### 3. Conceptual design
- Brainstorming
- Design thinking
- Evaluation of alternatives

### 4. Detailed engineering
- CAD assemblies
- Technical drawings
- Material selection
- Safety considerations

### 5. Technical calculations
Depending on the project:
- Stress analysis
- Power transmission sizing
- Motor/gearbox selection
- Fluid dynamics
- Thermal analysis
- Kinematics

### 6. Economic and technical feasibility
- Cost estimation
- Manufacturability
- Maintenance
- Life‑cycle analysis

### 7. Documentation and presentation
- Full technical report
- Drawings and diagrams
- Specifications
- Project defence

## Outcome

Students learn to analyse real industrial systems, apply engineering theory, and communicate solutions professionally.

# 3) Graphic Representation & Interpretation (Interpretació Gràfica)

This is a **highly practical module** focused on mastering the rules and standards of **technical drawing according to ISO norms**.
Students learn to communicate engineering ideas with precision, clarity and professional rigor.

## Core Skills

### 1. Standardization and ISO Norms
- Line types
- Dimensioning rules
- Tolerances and fits
- Title blocks (caixetins)
- Sections and projections

### 2. Extensive Use of AutoCAD (2D and 3D)
Students produce:
- Mechanical parts
- Assemblies
- Fully dimensioned drawings
- Exploded views

### 3. Transition to Autodesk Inventor
Due to SolidWorks license limitations, the department transitioned to **Autodesk Inventor**, creating a unique learning environment where both students and teachers learned the software from zero.

This improved adaptability and broadened CAD skills.

# 4) Water Network Project (Projectes d’Aigües)

This module introduces students to **hydraulic systems**, combining theory with practical demonstrators and industry‑based projects.

## Example Projects

- Manometric height demonstrator
- Pascal’s Law model
- Car‑wash foam generator
- Dual‑circuit series/parallel demonstrator
- Salt‑water chlorinator

## Project Development

As in previous modules, students complete:
- Market study
- Pre‑project
- Specifications
- Drawings

- Safety and quality documentation

## Practical Hydraulic Work

Students work directly with:
- Pipes and fittings
- Valves
- Flow meters
- Pumps
- Pressure and flow concepts

This module stands out for its strong connection to real hydraulic installations.

# 5) Tooling Design (Disseny d’Útils) — Sheet Metal Processing & Stamping

This module focuses on the design of **industrial tooling**, specifically tools used in **sheet metal processing and stamping**.

## Theoretical Foundations

Students study:
- Mechanical properties of sheet metals
- Stress and deformation
- Neutral fibre theory
- Cutting, bending and forming

## Practical Focus: Stamping Die Design

Students design a complete **stamping die**, including:
- Punch and die geometry
- Cutting clearances
- Bending radii
- Material flow
- Press force calculations

## Processes Covered

- Bending
- Rolling
- Deep drawing
- Cutting and punching

By the end, students understand how to design safe, efficient and manufacturable tooling.

# 6) Polymer Mold Design — Plastic Injection Molding

This module is dedicated to the **design of molds for polymer products**, focusing on injection molding.
It is a **theoretical module**, as mold manufacturing is not performed in the workshop, but the technical depth is significant.

## Topics Covered

### 1. Fundamentals of Polymer Processing
- Thermoplastics vs. thermosets
- Viscosity and flow
- Shrinkage and warpage

### 2. Mold Architecture
- Cavity and core
- Runners and gates
- Ejector systems
- Cooling channels
- Venting

### 3. Design Considerations
- Parting lines
- Draft angles
- Wall thickness
- Avoiding defects

### 4. CAD Modelling
Students create:
- Mold layouts
- Exploded views
- Sectional drawings

### 5. Industrial Applications
The module connects theory with real manufacturing scenarios, helping students understand how molds influence product quality and production efficiency.
