# About Me

I am an engineer driven by curiosity, creativity and a practical love for making things that work. My background spans **mechanical engineering**, **electronics**, **automation**, **additive manufacturing** and **applied artificial intelligence**. I approach problems from multiple angles: conceptual design, hands‑on prototyping, and iterative validation. I enjoy turning abstract requirements into robust, manufacturable solutions and teaching others to do the same.

Beyond technical skills I value **clear communication**, **teamwork**, and **adaptability**. I have worked in multicultural environments, supported international clients, and held customer‑facing roles that strengthened my ability to listen, explain and deliver with empathy. Whether I am designing a mechanical assembly, troubleshooting an electrical installation, guiding students in the workshop, or training an AI model, my working principle is the same: **identify the problem, design a practical solution, validate quickly, and improve continuously**.

## Professional focus and teaching

Since 2025 I have been working as a **Technical Instructor** at EMT Granollers. My teaching blends theory with project‑based learning and hands‑on workshop guidance. I supervise student projects that cover mechanical design, manufacturing processes, industrial systems and product development. In the workshop I work daily with three‑phase industrial machines, power and control systems, and I teach students how to diagnose, repair and document real faults safely and efficiently.

**Core teaching strengths**
- **Practical diagnostics**: three‑phase power, contactors, relays, protection devices.
- **Project management**: market analysis, specifications, budgeting and delivery.
- **CAD and manufacturing**: CAD modelling, DFM, prototyping and validation.
- **Standards and drawing**: ISO drawing standards, tolerancing and correct dimensioning.

**Observers’ perception (selected highlights)**
- **Primary roles (observers):** *Resource Investigator (IR)* and *Specialist (ES)* — energetic initiative and deep technical contribution.
- **Notable observer percentiles:** **Investigator of Resources (IR) 79**, **Specialist (ES) 60**, **Specialist / Investigator and Specialist‑adjacent roles rated consistently by multiple observers**.

**Combined interpretation**
- The combined profile indicates a **blend of idea generation, analytical judgement and specialist depth**. This combination suits roles that require innovation, early‑stage project development and technical leadership. Observers emphasise initiative and opportunity‑seeking; my self‑view emphasises creative problem solving and critical evaluation.

## Strengths, tendencies and practical implications

**Strengths (how I apply them)**
- **Idea generation (Plant/Cerebro):** I lead concept phases, propose alternative architectures and prototype multiple approaches before selecting the most robust solution.
- **Analytical judgement (Monitor Evaluator):** I apply evidence‑based trade‑offs, risk assessment and clear acceptance criteria in design reviews and validation.
- **Opportunity spotting (Resource Investigator):** I source new tools, suppliers and collaborations; I bring external ideas into projects and teaching.
- **Specialist depth:** I produce detailed technical documentation, calculations and manufacturing drawings when a project requires domain expertise.
- **Implementation orientation (Implementer traits present):** I convert ideas into process sheets, machining sequences and quality checks that make production repeatable.

**Tendencies to manage**
- **Follow‑through:** the report notes a tendency for interest to wane after the initial creative phase. I mitigate this by formalising handovers, defining milestones and partnering with colleagues who excel at execution.
- **Directness / challenge:** I can be perceived as blunt; I work on framing feedback constructively and on active listening.
- **Delegation and coordination:** I consciously assign roles, use project management tools and mentor students to take ownership.
